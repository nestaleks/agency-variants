Thirteen directions for the new tidgroup.es

Each concept is a complete, working, responsive homepage built from TID group’s real content: the five projects, 37 published apartments with plans, the 12 services, the company’s contacts and the positioning of the new printed catalog. They differ in layout, typography, colour, imagery and storytelling — not just in accent colour.

/design-concepts/concept-a/

Concept A · Digital adaptation of the catalog

Catálogo Vivo

The printed catalog, translated faithfully to the web: the dark-green diagonal corner with its gold keyline, S-curve image crops, italic serif chapter openers per project and the three catalog pillars. Corporate, warm and reassuring.

Type Cormorant Garamond italic + Jost

Positioning Established Mediterranean developer — continuity between print and web.

Advantages

  • One consistent brand across catalog, site and sales meetings
  • Project chapters with photo switcher, filter and apartment dialog with plans
  • Lowest risk; easiest for the owner to approve

Limitations

  • Least original — depends on the catalog’s look
  • Wave/diagonal motifs can feel decorative if overused

/design-concepts/concept-b/

Concept B · Bold editorial luxury

Atlas Editorial

A property magazine: oversized Bodoni headlines, a table of contents with image peek, each building as a numbered feature with its own spread layout, Spanish catalog lines as pull quotes, and a contact form written as a letter.

Type Bodoni Moda + Karla

Positioning Exclusive, design-literate buyers; “architecture with soul”.

Advantages

  • Most memorable, magazine-grade art direction
  • Every project gets its own spread and unit list
  • Terracotta echoes Mallorcan roofs and timber shutters

Limitations

  • Long page; needs strong photography for each feature
  • Diverges from the catalog’s green/gold identity

/design-concepts/concept-c/

Concept C · Contemporary digital

Investor Desk

A decision tool rather than a brochure: an apartment finder over all 37 published units (filters by project, bedrooms, area, outdoor space; sortable), a unit drawer with floor plan and “request this apartment”, project tabs and a buyer FAQ. Uses the existing logo cyan as its only accent.

Type Bricolage Grotesque + IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Mono

Positioning Investors and remote buyers comparing options.

Advantages

  • Clearest path to a qualified lead (unit pre-filled in the form)
  • Turns existing unit data into a real feature
  • Keeps the current logo colour — no rebrand needed

Limitations

  • Least aligned with the catalog’s warmth
  • Data must be kept accurate (prices, availability)

/design-concepts/concept-d/

Concept D · Minimalist architectural precision

Marès

Calm and exact, named after Mallorca’s sandstone. A visible 12-column hairline grid, one condensed variable typeface, dimension lines on photography, a works selector that toggles between photograph and architectural drawing, and every home drawn as a measured bar (interior + outdoor, to scale).

Type Archivo (variable width/weight) only

Positioning Architecture-led developer; quiet, expensive, deliberate.

Advantages

  • Very premium with little decoration; fast and maintainable
  • Shows plans and sizes honestly — strong for credibility
  • Ages well

Limitations

  • Less emotional; relies on visitors reading
  • Restraint may read as “cold” to lifestyle buyers

/design-concepts/concept-e/

Concept E · Expressive brand experience

Mediterráneo

A story told in the colours of the island: a word-by-word hero with parallax and a rising sun, a sticky four-chapter story “from the plot to the keys” whose background shifts tone, a draggable reel of homes opening into photo-story modals, and tabbed services.

Type Fraunces + Figtree

Positioning Lifestyle and second-home buyers; emotional first impression.

Advantages

  • Highest emotional impact; strong social/ads landing page
  • Explains the full-cycle offer as a story
  • All motion respects “reduce motion”

Limitations

  • Most complex to maintain; heavier on mobile
  • Less space for technical unit data

/design-concepts/concept-f/

Concept F · Warm organic · Mallorcan vernacular

Casa de Cal

A home that feels made by hand: limewashed walls, the round Mallorcan arch and green shutters. An arched hero window whose shutters swing open, projects as five arched doorways opening into “rooms”, and a compare tray that puts up to three apartments side by side.

Type Young Serif + Onest

Positioning Buyers who choose a home with their heart first; warm, tactile, local.

Advantages

  • Compare tray (up to 3 homes, any project) feeds the enquiry form
  • Most Mallorca-specific look — arches and shutters as a system
  • Every apartment and plan one or two clicks away

Limitations

  • Rustic tone may undersell the modern architecture of Marina and Foners
  • More decorative layers (masks, grain, fixed tray) to maintain

/design-concepts/concept-g/

Concept G · Neo-brutalist · builder’s site board

Obra

A construction-site board turned into a website: giant “OBRA”, tape-stripe ticker, hard-shadow spec-sheet cards that expand into unit tables, a zoom-and-pan floor-plan viewer, blueprint-grid process band and a dense icon grid of services. Says “we actually build”.

Type Unbounded + Instrument Sans + JetBrains Mono

Positioning The builder-developer you can check — every plan shown openly.

Advantages

  • Most transparent: every plan zoomable, every unit listed
  • Instantly distinct from all other directions
  • Strict, cheap-to-maintain system (few tokens, one shadow)

Limitations

  • Reads less “luxury”; far from the printed catalog
  • Very wide display type wraps a lot on mobile

Concept H · Cinematic dark luxury

Nocturno

The island after sunset, told like a hospitality brand film: a crossfading showreel hero with film-credit titles and pause control, each residence as a full-screen scene with closing letterbox bars, a cinematic lightbox for photos and plans, services as a credits roll and a “private viewing” request.

Type Italiana + Hanken Grotesk

Positioning Premium “private viewing” experience — desire first, details on demand.

Advantages

  • Premium first impression on par with B and D
  • Dusk grade unifies mixed renders into one series
  • Homes, plans and gallery one click from each scene

Limitations

  • Dark and image-led — far from the light catalog, less scannable
  • More motion and scrolling; heavier on low-end phones

/design-concepts/concept-i/

Concept I · Cartographic · organised by place

Carta Náutica

On an island the address is the asset. A nautical chart of TID’s Mallorca: a hand-drawn island map with contours and compass rose, an interactive Palma chart with project pins plus a Sóller locator, a synced gazetteer list and a “legend card” per building with location facts and units.

Type Newsreader + Sora + DM Mono

Positioning Buyers who choose by neighbourhood and distance to the sea.

Advantages

  • Makes location — the main value driver — the way to browse
  • Memorable identity, warm and Mediterranean
  • Units and pre-filled enquiry one click from the map

Limitations

  • Map is stylised; pin positions need owner confirmation
  • Little link to the printed catalog; more SVG/JS to maintain

/design-concepts/concept-j/

Concept J · Friendly product · bento grid

Hogar Match

For first-time and remote buyers who find luxury sites intimidating: a pastel bento hero, a 3-question home-match quiz (where, bedrooms, must-have) that ranks real apartments with “why it matches” chips, expandable project cards, filterable services and a plain-language investment FAQ.

Type Schibsted Grotesk + Literata italic

Positioning Guided, approachable path for first-time and remote buyers.

Advantages

  • Lowest barrier: a specific home in about four clicks
  • Every match leads to a pre-written enquiry for that unit
  • Honest about sold homes, no prices and partial matches

Limitations

  • Least premium; can read as a startup, not a boutique developer
  • Quiz value limited by only 37 published units

/design-concepts/concept-k/

Concept K · Retro Mallorca travel poster

Postal

Homes on the island sold the way 1960s Mallorca was sold to travellers: a poster hero of sun disc, mountains and wave bands, each building as a two-colour collectible poster that turns over into a postcard listing its homes, the process as postage stamps, services as tickets and the contact form as an airmail postcard.

Type Rozha One + Albert Sans + Courier Prime

Positioning Lifestyle and foreign buyers — the island first, the building second.

Advantages

  • Most memorable and joyful direction; strong for social and ads
  • Postcard dialog lists every home and leads straight to a prefilled enquiry
  • Pure CSS/SVG art — each project’s colour pair is a one-line change

Limitations

  • Playful tone weakens premium and investor perception; far from the catalog
  • Duotone photos hide real materials; no gallery or plans in the dialog

Concept L · Luminous water & light

Agua

Pools and the sea are real selling points, so the page reads like water: bright and glassy at the surface, darkening to deep pool as you scroll. A caustics-lit hero with pause control, glass project cards with a drag slider between render and architectural plan, and real-only “water” chips.

Type Syne + Mulish

Positioning Fresh lifestyle brochure — pool, beach and rooftop, nothing invented.

Advantages

  • Fresh high-key look, unlike any other concept
  • Render/plan slider shows real drawings in one gesture
  • Every project, home and service group prefills the form

Limitations

  • Little continuity with the green/gold catalog
  • Glass and canvas effects cost more to render and maintain

/design-concepts/concept-m/

Concept M · Annual report · private-bank dossier

Dossier

The website as an investment dossier: a report cover with contents, numbered sections and footnotes, SVG charts computed from all 37 published units (homes per building, area dot plot, bedroom mix), each building as a numbered exhibit with its unit schedule, and a printable dossier or one-page A4 per project.

Type Source Serif 4 + Public Sans

Positioning Investors and family offices who read the figures before they call.

Advantages

  • Strongest trust signal: every number traceable, caveats stated
  • Take-away one-pager per building without a backend
  • Charts and tables generated from data.js stay correct

Limitations

  • Least emotional; photography plays a supporting role
  • Long and dense on mobile; unit schedules scroll sideways

What the audit found

Strengths today

  • Real photos, renders and floor plans for all five projects
  • Unit-level data (areas, bedrooms, terraces)
  • EN/ES translation system
  • Simple static hosting

Weaknesses

  • Generic template look; teal/Poppins conflicts with the new green/gold catalog
  • Stock images (bitcoin, toy house, workers) in the core “What we do” block
  • No hero CTA, no contact form; key offer hidden behind “Read more”
  • Catalog trust facts (since 2018, 7 years, quality control) missing

Technical issues

  • $ is not defined on home and services (jQuery not loaded)
  • Broken footer logo path on root pages; wrong script paths on gallery page
  • Heading levels skip; many empty alt; Ukrainian tooltip on the English site
  • Likely placeholder prices repeated across three projects

Full reports are in the project repository: docs/03-website-audit.md, docs/01-catalog-analysis.md, docs/02-asset-inventory.md.

Recommendation

Build on Concept A — Catálogo Vivo, and bring in Concept C’s apartment finder as the projects section.

TID group has just invested in a printed catalog; the website should continue that identity so that a buyer moving from print to web recognises the brand immediately. A delivers that with the lowest risk. Its weakest point — a brochure that doesn’t help buyers choose — is exactly what C solves: the 37 published apartments become searchable, and every enquiry arrives with the project and unit attached. This is the best balance of aesthetics, credibility and conversion for a boutique developer selling 7–9 homes per building.

  • Most aligned with the catalog: A
  • Strongest distinctive identity: B (new set: F and I)
  • Clearest commercial focus: C (J for first-time buyers, M for investors)
  • Best balance of aesthetics and usability: A (with C’s finder)
  • Worth borrowing: D’s measured area bars for unit comparison · E’s “from the plot to the keys” story for the services page and ads · B’s numbered project features for individual project pages · F’s compare tray · G’s zoomable plan viewer · I’s map as a “Locations” page · J’s home-match quiz as an entry point for remote buyers · H’s showreel for campaign landing pages · L’s render/plan slider on project pages · M’s printable one-pager per building for sales meetings · K’s poster style for seasonal campaigns

Before production: confirm current prices/availability, social media links, Spanish copy for the new texts, and a form backend (e.g. a form service) to replace the email hand-off.
